Marine Spatial Planning Atlas for Kwale & Lamu Counties

Client: Nairobi Convention Secretariat / UNEP · GIS & Remote Sensing Specialist
PLATE 01 · 2026
PostGISArcGIS RESTPython ISO 19115/19139Marine & Coastal GIS

Built the geospatial backbone for Kenya's marine spatial plan across two coastal counties, pulling 17 thematic data layers (bathymetry, coral reefs, mangroves, seagrass, fisheries, marine protected areas, human uses) into a structured PostGIS database, then producing the thematic maps that planners and government officials actually use to make zoning decisions.

  • Digitised spatial features from planning documents, technical reports, and participatory mapping sessions with coastal communities.
  • Designed a production PostGIS schema (8 schemas, 33 spatial tables, spatial indexes and audit triggers) with a Python ingestion pipeline that auto-discovers and reprojects incoming datasets.
  • Ran the spatial analysis behind the plan: conflict detection between human uses and marine habitats, conservation-gap analysis, and coverage against Kenya's 30×30 biodiversity targets.
  • Authored ISO 19115/19139-compliant metadata for every dataset so the atlas integrates into the national MSP data catalogue.

Enterprise GIS Readiness & Roadmap for Njoro Kubwa Bulk Water Supply

Client: TAVEVO Water & Sewerage Co. Ltd, Taita Taveta County · GIS Specialist
PLATE 02 · 2026
GIS Readiness AssessmentStakeholder Engagement Non-Revenue WaterWater-Sector GIS

Before a water utility can build a GIS, someone has to answer an unglamorous question honestly: is the organisation actually ready for one? This engagement assessed TAVEVO's governance, data, people, and technology against enterprise-GIS adoption, then translated the findings into a phased, costed roadmap the utility's board could act on.

Assessment spanned consultations with TAVEVO's technical, commercial, finance, legal, ICT and HR departments, alongside external regulators and stakeholders including WASREB, WRA, WSTF, the Water Tribunal, county water and lands departments, and the Ministry of Water. It evaluated spatial data completeness, positional accuracy, and institutional capacity for enterprise adoption.

FleetPulse FMS

Independent project, in active development · Full-Stack Engineer & Spatial Systems Architect
PLATE 04 · 2026
Django / DRFPostGISMulti-Tenant SaaS MapLibre GLReact 19Redis

FleetPulse is a fleet and logistics platform I'm building from scratch, the whole system this time, not just the GIS layer on top of someone else's product. It's where the geospatial work I do for clients meets the full-stack engineering side: a multi-tenant backend, live GPS telematics, and the mapping and dashboards that turn a moving fleet into something a manager can actually see and act on.

Each company's fleet data is isolated twice over: once in the application layer, and again with PostgreSQL row-level security policies at the database itself, so one tenant's vehicles are never reachable from another's session, even by a query that tries to go around the app. Geofences are stored as real PostGIS polygons rather than approximations. Incoming GPS pings get deduplicated, resolved to the right vehicle even when a tracker was reassigned partway through, and streamed live to a MapLibre tracking map on the dashboard.

  • Multi-tenancy enforced independently at the application and database layers, with cross-tenant read and write attempts actually tested against a live Postgres instance rather than assumed safe.
  • A telematics pipeline handling event deduplication, time-bounded device-to-vehicle resolution, and a Redis-backed live-position store feeding the tracking map.
  • Fourteen domain modules so far, covering fleet and driver management, trip and stop detection, geofencing, fuel tracking, container and shipment logistics, driver safety reporting, and a client-facing portal.
  • 438 automated tests, including the cross-tenant isolation tests that matter most for a multi-tenant product.

Leading the geospatial planning for fishing boat mapping on Pemba Island. I inventoried 116 georeferenced aerial images and screened coastal coverage to assess whether the imagery could support a volunteer mapping and GeoAI data workflow.

  • Visual review of 25 images recorded 1,032 boat sightings; a conservative overlap adjustment gives at least 1,019 distinct visible boats in the reviewed imagery.
  • Preparing the next steps: a raster tile-serving test, a MapSwipe Locate Objects workflow, and a separate validated boat-level annotation process.
