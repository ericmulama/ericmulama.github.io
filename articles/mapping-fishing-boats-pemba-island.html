Mapping Fishing Boats on Pemba Island

How a coastal imagery review is shaping a proposed MapSwipe workflow and a future GeoAI training dataset.

Small fishing boats can be easy to miss in a broad coastal map. In high-resolution aerial imagery, they become visible one by one: pulled up on shore, moored near landing sites, or afloat close to the island. The challenge is turning those visible objects into trustworthy, reusable geographic data.

On Pemba Island, I am leading the geospatial planning for a fishing boat mapping project using drone imagery. The work is ongoing. So far, the central task has been to establish whether the available imagery contains enough discernible boats to support a volunteer mapping exercise and, later, a carefully validated dataset for GeoAI.

First, establish what the imagery can support

The local collection comprises 116 georeferenced GeoTIFF images. I screened their footprints against a coastline reference, then used QGIS to prioritize coastal images for visual review. Coastline proximity was only a screening clue: some apparently coastal image footprints proved to be inland when inspected.

Across 25 reviewed images, visual counts recorded 1,032 boat sightings. Most reviewed footprints did not overlap. One image overlapped a previously counted image containing 13 boats; even if all 13 were counted twice, the conservative minimum is 1,019 distinct visible boats. That clears the project's 1,000-boat visibility target. The counts are visual observations, not yet independently validated object labels or a completed training set.

Design MapSwipe around its actual output

The proposed next stage is a MapSwipe Locate Objects project. Volunteers would inspect small image tiles and indicate whether each contains no boats, one boat, or multiple boats. That can help focus attention across a large coastal area and bring more people into the mapping work.

MapSwipe's tile responses are not precise boat bounding boxes or outlines. To build an object-detection dataset, the team still needs an agreed boat-level annotation format, a process for resolving uncertain cases, and quality checks. A raster tile-serving test and an agreed area of interest are also needed before a MapSwipe campaign can begin.

From volunteer mapping to GeoAI

With validated boat-level labels, imagery and annotations could support GeoAI models that detect fishing boats at scale. That is the intended direction, not a result already delivered. Model training would require careful dataset packaging, attention to duplicate imagery, and evaluation against held-out areas so performance reflects real coastal conditions.

As project lead, my role spans imagery sourcing and preparation, the mapping workflow, coordination with collaborators, validation decisions, and eventual documentation and handover. The completed feasibility review gives the team a concrete starting point: visible boat volume is sufficient in the reviewed Pemba imagery. The remaining work is to turn that visibility into reliable labels and a reproducible mapping process.
